How to use the Baby Senses Lab

The lab turns decades of infant research into something you can try: pick an age and see, hear and check what babies typically manage at that point. It was built for curious parents, educators, students and researchers.

Not a medical test. This is a simulation and research tool built from published averages. Babies vary widely and develop at their own pace. If you are worried about a baby's seeing, hearing or development, talk to a paediatrician or health visitor.

Quick tour

  1. Pick an age on the start page. The age you choose follows you around the lab.
  2. Age profile: what babies typically see, hear, understand and do at that age, in six areas, with a comparison age and a printable page.
  3. See like a baby: a sample drawing, your own photo or your own video, shown as a baby of that age might see it.
  4. Hear like a baby: a sample sound, a recording of your voice or your own file, played as a baby might hear it.
  5. Activities and words: 27 everyday activities with the abilities they need, and a check for about 100 early words.
  6. What the models learned: the research results in plain words.
  7. Try with a baby: five short looking and listening activities with a real baby, with notes you can download.

Running a session with a baby

Read the set-up steps on the Try with a baby page. Keep sessions short (five to ten minutes), stop when the baby is tired or upset, and run each activity more than once on different days before reading anything into it. If you are collecting notes for research, get the family's informed consent and follow your institution's ethics rules.

Where the numbers come from

  • Vision: sharpness and contrast tables from the project plan (Norcia & Tyler 1985; Banks & Bennett 1988), plus acuity-card norms (Teller 1997; Mayer et al. 1995).
  • Hearing and language: hearing development (Werner 2007), speech-sound tuning (Werker & Tees 1984), name recognition (Mandel et al. 1995), early words (Bergelson & Swingley 2012; Wordbank, Frank et al. 2021).
  • Thinking and social: object permanence (Baillargeon 1987), gaze following and helping studies as summarised in the project plan.
  • Movement and 15-24 months: CDC/AAP milestone checklists (Zubler et al. 2022).

Every table is in docs/app/lab-data.js and can be checked or corrected.

Privacy

Everything runs in your browser. Photos, videos, recordings and session notes are never uploaded. Saved sessions live in this browser's storage until you delete them.

Feedback

Found something wrong, or tried it with a family? Open an issue on GitHub. Feedback from parents, educators and clinicians is exactly what phase 1 still needs.

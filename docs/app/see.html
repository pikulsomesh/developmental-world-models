How the view is made

Three changes are applied, each set by age. Blur removes detail finer than the baby's sharpness limit, worked out from how big the scene is and how far away it is. Contrast is reduced so faint edges fade. Colour is faded toward grey. These follow the same tables the paper's models use (code).

What it leaves out: babies also move their eyes differently, focus less well up close in the first weeks, and their brains fill in a great deal. The picture shows the limits of the eye, not the baby's experience.

The sample pictures are simple drawings made by this page, not photographs.

Not a medical test. This is a simulation and research tool built from published averages. Babies vary widely and develop at their own pace. If you are worried about a baby's seeing, hearing or development, talk to a paediatrician or health visitor.

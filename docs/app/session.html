Try with a baby

Five short activities you can run on a laptop or tablet with a baby on someone's lap. The screen shows the activity; you watch the baby and tap what you saw. At the end you get a summary and a file to keep.

This is an observation aid, not a screening or diagnostic test. Screens are not calibrated equipment, a single session is noisy, and tired or hungry babies simply look away. Results here cannot tell you whether a baby's vision or hearing is normal. If you have any concern, see a paediatrician, health visitor, optometrist or audiologist.

1. Set up

  • A quiet room, a calm and alert baby, sitting on a carer's lap facing the screen. The carer looks away from the screen or closes their eyes so they don't guide the baby.
  • Screen brightness comfortable, volume moderate. Nothing flashes; stop at any sign of upset.
  • The observer sits where they can see the baby's eyes, ideally not the screen. Use the buttons or the keyboard keys shown.
  • For the sound activity, place speakers to the baby's left and right; laptop speakers are too close together.
  • For babies born early, use their corrected age (age from the due date).

Measure the visible width of the screen with a ruler. These two numbers are used to size the stripes and the moving toy correctly.

Tool 4

Hide-and-seek

Infants look longer when a toy that went behind a screen fails to reappear. We show the models the same two movies and measure their surprise (one-step prediction error). A model that expects the toy to persist should be more surprised by the impossible movie.

Vision model, frame by frame

Surprise (mean squared prediction error) of one vision model on the two movies. The shaded band marks when the toy is hidden. A positive violation-of-expectation index needs the red line to lie above the blue line around the hidden stretch.

Which sense notices? The fused model, by event

Bars: mean violation-of-expectation index over seeds, whiskers ±1 s.d. Positive means more surprise at the impossible event.

The index

\mathrm{VoE} = \frac{S_{\text{imp}}-S_{\text{pos}}}{S_{\text{imp}}+S_{\text{pos}}}\in[-1,1]

Why vision models are not surprised by the impossible event is derived on Theory: a squared-error forecaster that is uncertain about when the toy reappears predicts a blend of screen and toy, and is therefore more surprised by reappearance.

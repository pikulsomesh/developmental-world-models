Baby Senses Lab

What can a baby see, hear and understand?

Babies' senses start out blurry, muffled and imprecise, and sharpen over the first two years, each on its own schedule. Pick an age to explore what babies typically sense and do, see a picture or hear a sound the way they might, and try simple looking and listening activities with a real baby.

A simulation and research tool, not a medical test. Everything here is built from published averages for groups of babies. It cannot tell you whether a particular baby's seeing, hearing or development is on track. If you have a concern, talk to a paediatrician or health visitor.

The research behind it

The lab grew out of a study asking whether computer models learn like babies when given the same blurry start. Short answer: sometimes. Read the paper.

Four findings

Each card answers one question about infants and machine learning. Values are means over independent training seeds at the 12-month checkpoint unless stated, read from the repository's result files. Every card links to its evidence.

Q1

Do senses mature in the infant order?

Only by construction

Vision develops last in the learned curves, but only because the input filters are set that way. A ceiling-based measure does not recover the infant order.

Evidence: input schedule and learning

Q2

Does starting with immature input help?

Partly

A borderline gain in hearing and no clear cost in vision. Network growth slowed learning and halved training compute.

Evidence: costs and benefits · growth

Q3

Do models expect hidden objects to persist?

No

No vision model was surprised when a hidden toy vanished. The fused model was, but only through the hearing channel.

Evidence: decodable is not expected · demo

Q4

Do combined senses beat any one sense?

Yes

A fully hidden object's position is best decoded from the fused model.

Evidence: fusion · charts

These are results for controlled synthetic models and a synthetic nursery; see Scope and limitations for what they do and do not support.

The study at a glance

4 + 1

sense world models (vision, hearing, touch, proprioception) and a fusion world model

6

training regimes crossing input schedule, network growth and two order controls

0 · 3 · 6 · 12

read-out checkpoints (months), stages of one continuous training run

– seeds

independent training seeds per regime for the sense models

Models range from 8,340 to 586,499 parameters per sense and are trained on CPU in a synthetic multisensory nursery: 32×32 colour vision, binaural hearing, an 8×8 tactile patch and proprioception, with a moving object, a babbling hand and an occluding screen. The Study design page gives the full protocol.

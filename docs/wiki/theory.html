Theory

The same results read through three disciplines: mathematics (what bounds and explains them), computer science (what to do with them) and neuroscience (what the models stand for and where the analogy stops).

Mathematics

Front ends bound what can be learned

Let Y be a property of the scene (the toy's position) and \tilde O_a=\Phi_s(O;a) the filtered observation. For any learned representation Z=e(\tilde O_a), the data-processing inequality gives

I(Z;Y)\le I(\tilde O_a;Y)\le I(O;Y).

The supervised information ceiling estimates the middle term. The gap between ceiling and model is a property of learning; the ceiling itself is a property of the input.

Why a squared-error forecaster is not surprised

The minimiser of \mathbb E\lVert \hat o_{t+1}-o_{t+1}\rVert^2 over predictors of the past is the conditional mean \hat o_{t+1}^\star=\mathbb E[o_{t+1}\mid o_{\le t}]. Behind a screen, let the toy reappear at frame t+1 with probability p. At each pixel the optimal forecast is a blend \hat o=(1-p)\,o^{\text{screen}}+p\,o^{\text{toy}}, and the expected surprise is

S_{\text{reappear}}=(1-p)^2\Delta^2,\qquad S_{\text{vanish}}=p^2\Delta^2,\qquad \Delta=o^{\text{toy}}-o^{\text{screen}}

so a model is more surprised by reappearance whenever p<\tfrac12, which holds for most frames of an occlusion of uncertain length. A negative violation-of-expectation index is the expected behaviour of a well-trained pixel forecaster, not a failure of training. Try it on the hide-and-seek demo.

Function-preserving growth and its delay

Widen a layer from n to n' units, W_1'=\begin{bmatrix}W_1\\R\end{bmatrix}, W_2'=[\,W_2\;\;0\,]. Then W_2'\phi(W_1'x)=W_2\phi(W_1x) for every x. The gradient on the new incoming weights is

\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial R}=\big(\mathbf 0^\top\,\delta\big)\odot\phi'(Rx)\,x^\top=0,

so new units cannot learn until their outgoing weights move away from zero, which takes a number of updates that grows with the inverse of the learning rate. The origin of this zero-gradient start is a saddle that the optimiser must leave. The grown models pay this cost at each of the three growth events.

Computer science

  • Curriculum as continuation. Immature input is a curriculum in the sense of continuation methods: optimise a smoothed objective first, then track its minimiser as the smoothing is removed.
  • Augmentation versus order. In hearing, shuffled input quality is close to the immature schedule and both exceed adult input, which suggests that the benefit is mostly augmentation by degraded input rather than order.
  • Growth trades accuracy for compute. At matched updates, growth costs a fraction of the parameter-updates of a fixed-size network and delays learning (Results).
  • Late fusion. Late fusion of separately trained senses avoids the competition between modalities seen under joint training, a known problem in multimodal learning; modality dropout trains cross-modal fill-in.
  • Probes versus paradigms. A read-out can show that information about a hidden object is present while the model's predictions do not use it; object permanence has to be tested with paradigms, not probes.

Neuroscience

Each front end stands for a peripheral or early stage that matures on its own clock. The brain adds synapses and then prunes them (Huttenlocher, 1997) rather than adding neurons, and closes plasticity on per-sense schedules governed by excitatory-inhibitory balance (Hensch, 2005). Our growth ablation is therefore a test of an engineering idea, not of biology; the biologically motivated levers are sensory immaturity and plasticity schedules.
